EXPERIMENT 2- Error analysis

Objectives:
Test a simple regex-based tokenizer on linguistically tricky cases and compare its output against a gold-standard tokenization to determine where naive tokenization breaks down.

Test Cases:
The tokenizer was tested on:
- Abbreviations
- Decimal numbers
- URLs 
- Email addresses
- Hyphenated expressions
- Contractions 
- Uppercase abbreviations

Observed Problems:
- Predicted 59 tokens vs 38 gold tokens, a 55% over-segmentation.
- Abbreviations split on the period for Dr. vs Dr + .
- Decimal numbers broken at the decimal point
- URLs shredded into fragments
- Email addresses split at each hyphen.
- Contractions split incorrectly.

Research observation:
All errors stem from the same root cause: the regex tokenizer treats ".", "-", "@", ":", "/", "'" as universal delimiters, without context-sensitivity. It cannot distinguish a sentence-final period from a decimal point, abbreviation marker, or URL/email component; the same character plays different roles depending on surrounding context. This confirms tokenization is not a purely mechanical operation but requires linguistic and domain knowledge.
