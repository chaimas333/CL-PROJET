EXPERIMENT 3- Standard NLP tokenization

Objectives:
Compare the custom regex tokenizer /from exp2) against a standard NLP library tokenizer and the gold standard to quantify how much a pretrained tokenizer improves on the error categories identified earlier.

Test Cases:
The tokenizer was tested on:
- Abbreviations
- Decimal numbers
- URLs 
- Email addresses
- Hyphenated expressions
- Contractions 
- Uppercase abbreviations

Observed Problems:
- Both tokenizers still over-segment relative to golf (59 and 43 vs. 38).
- NLTK correctly handles abbreviations, decimals, and hyphenated compounds but fails on URLs, emails, and contractions.
- The regex tokenizer fails on every category tested; none of the special cases are handled.
- Positional match rate is a strict metric: since token counts diverge from golf early (splits shift all subsequent positions), it understates real improvement; many "mismatches" are actually correct tokens shifted by one position.

Research observation:
NLTK's rule-based/statistical tokenizer improves substantially over naive regex (39.47% vs. 5.26%), confirming that a general-purpose tokenizer encodes more linguistic knowledge than a hand-written pattern. However, it still lacks robust handling of URLs and emails, domain-specific token types not well covered by its general English-text training. This shows that even "standard" tokenizers require task-specific customization for specialized text.
